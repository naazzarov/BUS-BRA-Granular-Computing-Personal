# Does image disagreement help prioritize review?

Analysis plan written before computing the new endpoint. Study design is evaluation of patient-level prediction deferral on two-image BUS-BRA groups. Question and feature use differ from the completed feature-selection experiment. The locked handoff is the input. Use all numeric predictors, image-level scores, one diagnosis per patient group, and the supplied five outer folds. The pair is defined by patient group and the count of available image records.

Primary comparison is an error-ranking model trained on pair-average confidence plus absolute disagreement versus the same model trained on pair-average confidence. The image-level backbone is fixed L2 logistic regression. Histogram gradient boosting is a fixed sensitivity analysis. The backbone trains on all eligible training patients with total weight one per patient. To train the error-ranking model, make image predictions in three patient-grouped inner folds and collapse them to patient-level scores. Refit only the image model on all outer training patients. Test both deferral rules on identical two-image patients and base decisions. Use a 0.5 decision threshold. Plain margin and minimum confidence across images are descriptive comparators.

Primary outcome is area under the generalized risk–coverage curve (AUGRC). Compare fold-mean AUGRC for the two learned rules through a paired patient bootstrap within folds, conditional on fixed fits. Use 5,000 draws and seed 20261124. Report risk at observed 80% and 90% coverage, exact coverage counts, and retention by diagnosis at 90% as secondary descriptive endpoints. Include single-image groups only in descriptive full-coverage analysis. Show evidence for both backbones without selecting a better-performing one afterward. Retain the existing fold structure for comparability.
